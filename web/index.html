Method

Methodology

All figures on this dashboard are derived from the match scores published on LeagueApps. A statistical model estimates a strength rating for each team from those scores. The ratings determine the probability of a win, draw or loss in every remaining match, and the remainder of the season is simulated 25,000 times from those probabilities. Points already earned carry into every simulation; only unplayed matches are simulated.

Ratings

The power rating is a team's expected goal margin per match against a league-average opponent, offset by 5 so that the league average is 5.0. A rating of 6.0 therefore corresponds to an expected margin of +1 goal per match against an average team, and 4.0 to −1. Ratings are opponent-adjusted: each result is evaluated relative to the opponent's strength, so a one-goal loss to the strongest team can imply a higher rating than a one-goal win over the weakest.

The range beneath each rating is an approximate 80% interval for the team's strength relative to the league average. Intervals are widest for teams with the fewest rated matches. Teams whose intervals overlap substantially cannot be reliably separated, whatever their rank order.

Three rating models are available. The selector at the top of the page determines which model drives every rating, forecast and simulation on the dashboard.

Match forecasts

In the two margin-based models, the difference between two teams' ratings is the expected goal margin of their match. The realized margin is modelled as normally distributed around that expectation, with a standard deviation calibrated to the league's observed draw frequency. The probabilities that the margin rounds to a positive whole number, to zero, or to a negative whole number are the win, draw and loss probabilities. The Dixon-Coles model instead estimates each team's expected goals, assigns a probability to every possible scoreline, and sums those probabilities by outcome.

No home advantage is modelled, because every match is played at the same venue.

Season simulation

Each simulated season begins from the current official standings and samples a result for every remaining scheduled match. Teams are then ordered by points, goal difference and goals conceded, the tiebreak order used by LeagueApps. Because earned points are retained, a team with a modest rating and a strong record can hold a substantial playoff probability.

The top four teams qualify for the playoffs. First plays fourth, second plays third, and the two winners contest the final. A drawn playoff match is resolved with equal probability for each side. For every simulated final table, the probability of each playoff outcome is computed exactly, without further random draws.

The clinched and eliminated labels consider points only and resolve every tie on points against the team in question, since goal difference cannot be known in advance. They are exact once ten or fewer matches remain and conservative before that.

The schedule is not a complete round robin: each team omits one possible opponent. Projections include only scheduled matches. The Full round robin option on the Standings race tab adds the omitted matches, which shows how much the unplayed pairings affect each team's outlook.

Forecast evaluation

Accuracy is measured out of sample. Each completed match is predicted again from results on earlier dates only, reproducing the forecast that would have been available before kickoff. Each prediction is scored by its log loss, the negative natural logarithm of the probability it assigned to the actual result. Lower is better: a forecast that gave the actual result a probability of 1/2 scores 0.693, and one that gave it 1/3 scores 1.099. The reference forecast, labelled coin flip, ignores team identity. It assigns the season's draw rate to date, with a 17% prior, to a draw and splits the remainder equally between the two teams.

Goal-margin parameters were selected on 2026-10-09 and the parameters of the other two models on 2026-10-16. In each case, parameters were chosen on six completed NYC Footy leagues and evaluated on six different leagues.

The weekly recap on the Matches tab uses the same pre-match forecasts. An upset is a win by the team with the lower win probability. A draw is scored as half a win for each team and is therefore never classified as an upset, although any particular draw is improbable.

Additional details

The administrative forfeit of September 3 counts in the official standings but carries no weight in any rating.

To limit the influence of lopsided results, margins are capped at 4 goals in the goal-margin model and at 2 in the results + margin model, and the Dixon-Coles model caps each team's goals at 4 per match. A single large result can still move the rating of a team with few matches substantially; its interval and its match list on the Teams tab indicate by how much.

Consistency, shown on the Teams tab, is the root-mean-square difference in goals between a team's capped results and the margins its current rating predicts. Because current ratings are applied to every past match, it is a retrospective measure.

Forecasts use point estimates of team ratings and do not incorporate the uncertainty in those estimates.

In the result graph, each edge points from the winner to the loser, and its width increases with the logarithm of the goal margin. Teams linked by a cycle of results (A beat B, B beat C, C beat A) are grouped, because no consistent order exists among them. Dominance flow is a résumé measure: each team begins with one unit of credit, retains 40% and passes 60% to the teams that defeated it, weighted toward larger wins. It is disabled while any cycle exists.
