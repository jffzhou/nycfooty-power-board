Method

Methodology

Everything here comes from the scores on LeagueApps. A model rates each team from those scores, the ratings give win, draw and loss probabilities for every remaining match, and the rest of the season is simulated 25,000 times. Points already earned carry into every simulation.

Ratings

The power rating is a team's expected goal margin against a league-average opponent, plus 5. A 6.0 team should beat an average team by one goal a game. Ratings are opponent-adjusted, so a one-goal loss to the best team can rate higher than a one-goal win over the worst.

The 80% range is the uncertainty in a team's strength relative to the league average. It is widest for teams with few rated matches. Teams whose ranges overlap heavily cannot be reliably separated.

The selector at the top chooses one of three models for the whole dashboard.

Match forecasts

In the two margin models, the rating difference is the expected goal margin. The actual margin is modelled as normal around it, with a spread set by the league's draw rate; a positive, zero or negative rounded margin is a win, draw or loss. Dixon-Coles instead estimates each side's expected goals and adds up the scoreline probabilities. There is no home advantage, since every match is at the same field.

Season simulation

Each simulated season starts from the current standings and samples every remaining scheduled match. Teams are ordered by the league's tiebreakers: points, goal difference, goals scored, then head-to-head points, goal difference and goals among the tied teams, then a coin flip.

The top four make the playoffs: 1st plays 4th, 2nd plays 3rd, and the winners meet in the final. A drawn playoff match counts as 50/50. Playoff odds are computed exactly for each simulated table.

Clinched and eliminated use points only and break every tie against the team in question. They are exact with ten or fewer matches left and conservative before that.

Each team skips one possible opponent. Projections use only scheduled matches; the Full round robin option adds the missing ones.

Forecast evaluation

Each completed match is predicted again from results on earlier dates only and scored by log loss: the negative log of the probability given to the actual result. Lower is better; 1/2 scores 0.693 and 1/3 scores 1.099. The coin flip ignores who is playing: it gives a draw the season's draw rate so far (with a 17% prior) and splits the rest evenly.

Settings were chosen on six completed NYC Footy leagues and tested on six others: goal margin on 2026-10-09, the other two on 2026-10-16.

The weekly recap uses these same pre-match forecasts. An upset is a win by the less likely winner; a draw is never an upset.

Other details

The September 3 forfeit counts in the standings but not in any rating.

Margins are capped at 4 goals (goal margin) and 2 (results + margin); Dixon-Coles caps each side at 4 goals. One big result can still move a team with few matches a lot.

Consistency (Teams tab) is the root-mean-square gap between a team's capped margins and what its current rating predicts. It applies today's ratings to past matches, so it is retrospective.

Forecasts use point estimates of the ratings and ignore their uncertainty.

In the result graph, arrows run from winner to loser and get wider with the margin. Teams in a cycle (A beat B, B beat C, C beat A) are grouped, since no order fits them. Dominance flow gives each team one unit of credit; it keeps 40% and passes 60% to the teams that beat it, weighted toward bigger wins. It is off while any cycle exists.
